## What I do

- Keep circuit diagrams up to date with the code that drives them.
- Document every connection: pin, signal, and voltage.
- Provide a wiring table alongside each schematic for quick reference.

## When to use me

Use me whenever hardware changes: a new sensor, servo, audio module, or any
change to pin assignments, power, or wiring.

## Required for every schematic

- Wiring shown as a **breadboard picture** (top view with parts and coloured
  wires), generated by `tools/breadboard`; never a flow chart or mind map.
- The assembly guide follows the LEGO-booklet style: one picture per step, the
  new part highlighted, earlier parts kept in place, understandable by a
  12-year-old.
- A wiring table with columns: `Component | Pico pin | Signal | Voltage`.
- Power budget: how the board, servo, and audio module are powered.
- Common ground explicitly shown.

## Rules

- Never leave a pin assignment undocumented in both schematic and code.
- Keep firmware pin constants in sync with the documented wiring table.
- Note any voltage-level shifting or protection components.
- Flag anything that could draw more current than the supply provides.
- Rebuild the PDFs with `make schematics`; keep source and PDF in sync.
